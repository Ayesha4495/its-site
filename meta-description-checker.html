How the tool works

Meta checker: keep your result from getting cut off

A meta title and description are the two lines people read before deciding whether to visit your page. Google shows roughly the first 60 characters of a title and about 155 to 160 characters of a description, but the real limit is pixel width: around 580px for the title and 920px for the description on desktop. A title full of wide letters like W and M gets cut sooner than one with narrow letters like i and l, which is why we show both numbers.

Paste your current title and description into the boxes. The bar turns green when you are in the ideal range, amber when you are a little short, and red when you are over. The preview at the top of the page shows how the result will look, including the trailing dots when it gets trimmed.

Put the main keyword near the start of the title, and write the description like a short promise rather than a list of keywords. Give every page its own pair. Google sometimes rewrites descriptions to match a search, but a clear one is still chosen often, and a good title almost always stays. The most common mistakes are copying one description across many pages, leaving it empty, and writing so much that the best part is cut off.

Why does my title look shorter in Google than the character count says?

Google trims by pixel width, not by characters, so titles with wide letters get cut earlier.

Will Google always use my meta description?

Not always. Google may rewrite it to match the search, but a clear, relevant description is used more often.
