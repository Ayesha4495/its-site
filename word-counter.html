How the tool works

Word counter: write to the right length

Length matters more than most writers admit. A product description, a school essay, a social caption and a blog post all have different sweet spots, and checking while you write saves editing later. The counter shows words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and an estimated reading time based on 200 words a minute, a comfortable pace for most adults.

For SEO, a guide that fully answers a question often beats a thin page, but length alone does not rank. A 600-word page that solves the problem will usually beat a 2,000-word page that circles around it. Use the count to judge coverage, then cut anything that does not help the reader. Character counts are handy for tweets, SMS, ad headlines and app store listings, which all have strict limits.

Reading time helps you decide whether a long article needs a table of contents or shorter sections. You can paste text straight from Word or Google Docs. Hyphenated words count as one word, and numbers count as words. Because everything is counted in your browser, private drafts stay private.

How is reading time calculated?

We divide the word count by 200 words per minute and round up to the next minute.

How many words should a blog post have?

There is no fixed number. Write as many as it takes to answer the question well, often 800 to 1,500 for a full guide.
